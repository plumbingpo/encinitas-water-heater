Clear Pricing

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Before we start any repair, we figure out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians look at your heater's age and gallon capacity, the burner or elements, the relief valve, the venting, and any signs of corrosion or seepage. Then you get a straightforward recommendation and the full price up front, whether the job is swapping a thermocouple or replacing the whole unit. No hidden fees, no surprise add-ons.

The trouble with water heater failures is that many of them start out of sight. Hard-water minerals build a crust on the tank floor, the anode rod gets eaten away a little at a time, and once it's gone, the tank itself starts to corrode. If the water turns rusty, the tank rumbles, or a small drip appears at the base, get it looked at while it's still a simple repair.

Not every call is an emergency. Plenty of homeowners call us to size up an aging heater before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical tank lifespan of 8 to 12 years (tankless units often run 20 years with annual descaling), a replacement on your own timeline beats scrambling for a new heater with water on the floor. We'll give you a straight answer on how much life your unit has left.

Maintenance Plans

Tank Flushing and Tune-Ups That Pay Off

The best repair is the one you never need. That's the idea behind our maintenance plans. On each scheduled visit, we flush the sediment out of the tank, measure the anode rod, exercise the relief valve, look over the venting and gas connections or the electrical components, and descale tankless heat exchangers, all tailored to the equipment in your home.

No two maintenance plans look exactly alike. An older tank gets a closer look than a brand-new one, a tankless unit on hard water calls for regular descaling to protect its heat exchanger working, and a large family works the heater far harder than a couple does. We build each visit around your equipment and water, so every service keeps the unit efficient and protects the equipment you've already paid for.

The savings add up quickly. A flushed tank heats water with less effort, a replaced anode rod can add years to the tank, and a tested relief valve is ready to do its job if pressure ever climbs too high. Next to the cost of an early replacement, a maintenance plan is a bargain.

Proper Sizing

A Water Heater Sized for Your Actual Household

When it comes to water heaters, bigger is not automatically better. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, how many gallons of hot water it can deliver in one busy hour, and for a tankless unit it's the flow rate it can sustain at your incoming water temperature. That's why we never just copy the size of the old unit. Instead, we look at how many people live in the home, the number of bathrooms, plus large draws such as a jetted tub or high-capacity washer.

Households change over time. A growing family or an added bathroom can outgrow the tank that once kept up just fine. Tankless water heaters heat water on demand and don't run out as long as you stay within the rated flow, while hybrid heat pump tanks use far less electricity than a standard electric tank. Whether you're comparing tank and tankless, or gas and electric, we'll explain the honest pros and cons in upfront cost, operating cost, venting and gas line needs, and installation work.

Repairs We Handle

Hot Water Problems We Solve Every Week

Homes in Encinitas run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Encinitas Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that never gets hot enough, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks with burned-out elements, noisy tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

Some failures can wait a day, and some can't. A bad element just cuts your hot water supply. A relief valve that discharges, on the other hand, signals that pressure or temperature inside the tank is too high, and water pooling under the tank can quietly ruin subfloors and drywall before anyone notices. Smell gas? Leave the house and call your gas utility before anything else. We come prepared to replace elements, thermostats, thermocouples, gas valves, and relief valves, add a thermal expansion tank when pressure is the culprit, and handle leak repair at the drain valve or fittings.
