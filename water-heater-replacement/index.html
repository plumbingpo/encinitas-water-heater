Warning Signs to Watch For

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Higher gas or electric bills for less hot water
  • Repeated service calls on the same aging heater
  • Water seeping out from under the tank jacket
  • Sediment noise that keeps getting louder
  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections
  • Rusty or brown water from the hot side only
  • Hot water running out faster every month
  • A heater older than most tanks last, usually eight to twelve years

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

A water heater replacement involves more systems than most people expect. Gas work covers the supply line, sediment trap, shutoff, and a vent that must draft well enough to keep exhaust out of the house. Electrical work means matching breaker and wire gauge to the element wattage. The tank needs a relief valve of the correct rating piped to a safe discharge point, and depending on your home, code may require a thermal expansion tank, a drain pan, earthquake straps, or a permit and inspection. Tankless and heat pump models pile on more: bigger gas lines, sealed vent systems, heavy electrical loads, and condensate drainage.

What you get from a pro is a replacement that is done once. The new heater is matched to your demand, the gas supply, venting, and panel are confirmed, and the permit is handled if your jurisdiction requires one. We route the relief valve discharge correctly, add the code items that apply, test for leaks and proper draft, and take the old tank with us. Rushed swaps tend to leave missing expansion tanks, bad vent pitch, and undersized gas lines behind. Ours are done to code and to last. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here are our recommendations:

  • Keep household water pressure in a safe range to protect the tank and its fittings
  • Start a flushing routine in the first year, before sediment hardens
  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
  • Avoid running the tank hotter than about 120 degrees unless there is a specific need
  • Clean the air filter on a heat pump water heater every few months
  • Write the install date on the tank so you know when to plan the next replacement
  • Inspect the anode rod every few years; replacing it can add years to the tank
