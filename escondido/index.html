Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Escondido

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year in hard-water areas.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the manufacture date, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that range and is also showing rusty water, rumbling, or moisture at the base, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement once the tank fails.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a flooded utility room.
